Gravitational Potential Energy (GPE)

While Kinetic Energy ($KE$) is the energy of motion, Potential Energy is stored energy. The most common form we study is Gravitational Potential Energy ($GPE$), which is the energy an object possesses due to its vertical position (height) relative to a reference level.

$$GPE = m \cdot g \cdot h$$
  • $m$ = Mass of the object (in $\text{kg}$)
  • $g$ = Acceleration due to gravity ($\approx 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$ on Earth)
  • $h$ = Height above the reference level (in $\text{m}$)

App 1: Calculating GPE

A $2\text{ kg}$ book sits on a shelf $3\text{ m}$ above the floor. What is its GPE relative to the floor?

$GPE = 2 \times 9.8 \times 3$

$GPE = 58.8\text{ J}$

App 2: Negative GPE?

If the reference level ($h=0$) is the table, and a $1\text{ kg}$ object falls $1\text{ m}$ below the table, $h = -1\text{ m}$.

$GPE = 1 \times 9.8 \times (-1)$

$GPE = -9.8\text{ J}$

The Work-Kinetic Energy Theorem

The Work-Kinetic Energy Theorem establishes a direct relationship between the net work done on an object and the change in its kinetic energy. If work is done on a system, its kinetic energy increases or decreases exactly by that amount of work.

$$W_{\text{net}} = \Delta KE = KE_f - KE_i$$

For a Falling Object: As an object falls freely, the only force doing work on it is gravity (Weight). The positive work done by gravity causes the object's speed (and thus its Kinetic Energy) to increase.

App 3: Applying the Work-KE Theorem to a Falling Ball

A $1\text{ kg}$ ball is dropped from rest ($v_i = 0$) from a height of $5\text{ m}$. What is its velocity just before it hits the ground?

  • 1. Work done by Gravity ($W_g$):
    $W_g = F \cdot d = (m \cdot g) \cdot d = 1 \times 9.8 \times 5 = 49\text{ J}$
  • 2. Work-KE Theorem:
    $W_{\text{net}} = \Delta KE \implies W_g = KE_f - KE_i$
    $49 = KE_f - 0 \implies \mathbf{KE_f = 49\text{ J}}$
  • 3. Final Velocity ($v_f$):
    $49 = \frac{1}{2}(1)v_f^2 \implies v_f = \sqrt{98} \approx \mathbf{9.9\text{ m/s}}$
