Part I: Work Done by a Force $\vec{F}$

The mathematical definition of Work is the dot product of the Force vector and the Displacement vector. The SI unit for work is the Joule (J).

$$W(\vec{F}) = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d} = F \cdot d \cdot \cos\alpha$$

App 1: Motive Work

Calculate work done by $F=50\text{ N}$ moving $d=2\text{ m}$ at $\alpha=60^\circ$.

$W(\vec{F}) = 50 \times 2 \times \cos(60^\circ)$

$W(\vec{F}) = 50\text{ J}$

App 2: Resistive Work

Calculate work done by friction force $f=10\text{ N}$ on $d=3\text{ m}$. ($\alpha = 180^\circ$)

$W(\vec{f}) = 10 \times 3 \times \cos(180^\circ)$

$W(\vec{f}) = -30\text{ J}$

App 3: Zero Work

Calculate work done by Weight $W=40\text{ N}$ moving horizontally $d=5\text{ m}$.

$W(\vec{W}) = 40 \times 5 \times \cos(90^\circ)$

$W(\vec{W}) = 0\text{ J}$

Case Study: The Dumbbell Workout

Lifting Upwards

When you lift a weight, motion is UP.

  • Your arm pulls UP ($\alpha=0^\circ$) → Motive Work.
  • Weight acts DOWN ($\alpha=180^\circ$) → Resistive Work.

Lowering Downwards

When you lower a weight, motion is DOWN.

  • Your arm pushes UP to support it ($\alpha=180^\circ$) → Resistive Work.
  • Weight acts DOWN ($\alpha=0^\circ$) → Motive Work.

Part II: Kinetic Energy (KE)

$$KE = \frac{1}{2} m v^2$$

App 4: Calculating KE

Calculate the KE of a mass $m=2\text{ kg}$ moving at $v=3\text{ m/s}$.

$KE = \frac{1}{2}(2)(3)^2 \implies \mathbf{KE = 9\text{ J}}$

App 5: Solving for Velocity & Mass

Using algebraic manipulation on the KE formula:

$v = \sqrt{\frac{2KE}{m}}$

$m = \frac{2KE}{v^2}$

Part III & IV: Total Work & The Work-Energy Theorem

Total work ($W_{total}$) is the sum of the work done by all forces acting on an object. The Work-Energy Theorem states that this total net work is exactly equal to the change in the object's Kinetic Energy.

$$W_{total} = \Delta KE = KE_f - KE_i$$

App 6: Calculating $W_{total}$ on a Crate

A crate is pulled by $F=100\text{ N}$ at $37^\circ$. Friction force $f=20\text{ N}$, mass $m=10\text{ kg}$, horizontal distance $d=5\text{ m}$.

  • $W(\vec{F}) = 100 \times 5 \times \cos(37^\circ) \approx 400\text{ J}$
  • $W(\vec{f}) = 20 \times 5 \times \cos(180^\circ) = -100\text{ J}$
  • $W(\vec{N}) = W(\vec{W}) = 0\text{ J}$ (since motion is horizontal)

$W_{total} = 400 - 100 + 0 + 0 = \mathbf{300\text{ J}}$

App 7: Applying the Theorem

From App 6, if the crate starts from rest ($KE_i = 0$), what is its final velocity $v_f$ after $5\text{ m}$?

$W_{total} = KE_f - 0 \implies 300 = \frac{1}{2}(10)v_f^2$

$300 = 5v_f^2 \implies v_f = \sqrt{60} \implies \mathbf{v_f \approx 7.74\text{ m/s}}$
